System overview — LexiLite

How a contract turns into a risk report

A legal clause goes in as plain text; a label — safe, risky, or "ask a human" — comes out. The whole project is the machinery in between: a classifier trained on 13,823 labeled clauses, a confidence gate that refuses to guess when it isn't sure, and a Django API that serves the result in one request.

Why the gate exists

The training data itself explains the design choice — it's heavily skewed toward "safe."

safe · 11,756
2,067

85.1% safe / 14.9% risky across 13,823 labeled clauses (CUAD dataset). A classifier trained on this split can reach high raw accuracy just by leaning toward "safe" — which is exactly the failure mode the confidence gate is built to catch: a clause that's actually risky but gets a confident, wrong "safe."

The gate in analyzer/confidence.py checks two things, not one — min_confidence (the winning probability must clear 0.65) and min_margin (it must beat the runner-up by at least 0.15). The margin check exists because a 0.51-vs-0.49 call would still pass a bare confidence threshold, and a coin-flip prediction dressed up as "51% confident" is worse than admitting uncertainty.
